Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: transparent pixel-art tree sprite atlas for an interactive coastal garden RPG.
Primary request: ONE production-style atlas of TEN separate tree sprites. Exactly FIVE equal-width columns and TWO equal-height rows. Each column is one botanical tree type; row two is an alternative younger / more irregular version of that same type. All sprites must be fully inside their cells with ample transparent gutters, clearly separated, no overlap.
Style: polished retro top-down 3/4 pixel game art, closer to a lush late handheld RPG than realism. Chunky coherent leaf clusters, 2–3 shade steps per material, deliberate crisp stepped contours, roughly 80–120 native pixels per tree enlarged with nearest-neighbor. Leaf masses and trunks should read clearly at small game size. Dense and dimensional but restrained individual-pixel noise. No smooth painting, no blur, no vector gradients.
Camera: elevated three-quarter view for a square top-down garden grid, with a little visible top of the foliage and a visible trunk. One consistent camera and scale throughout. Light from upper left, warmer fresh green highlights, rich blue-teal underside shade. Trees are rooted at a single readable bottom-center anchor.
Composition: landscape 5:2 aspect ratio, five columns by two rows, ten tree cutouts. Row one mature, row two a distinct younger/irregular variant. Same column types in both rows:
Column 1 CHÊNE: a broad deciduous oak, rounded irregular spreading crown made of large overlapping leaf masses, stout gnarled grey-brown branching trunk, low sturdy branches, saturated leafy green and warm yellow-green top planes.

Column 3 PIN PARASOL: unmistakable wide, relatively flat umbrella-shaped crown, long exposed forked russet trunk, several broad green canopy pads joined into an umbrella, dark teal underside and lime sunlit tips.
Column 4 CHÊNE VERT: a compact rounded evergreen oak, dense dark olive-green small-leaf crown with several domed clumps, sturdy textured charcoal-brown trunk, some lighter grey-green edge highlights. Distinctly darker and more compact than the deciduous oak.
Column 5 BOULEAU: slender white birch trunk and pale branches with irregular dark bark markings, airy crown with small hanging leafy groups and visible gaps, bright fresh yellow-green light on top, some delicate side branches, clearly lighter and more open than the oaks.
Row 2 must keep the same five species but give each a different silhouette and trunk branching: a young oak with a leaning branch, a slightly crooked maritime pine, a smaller irregular umbrella pine, a multi-domed young holm oak, and a young birch with a forked white trunk.
Include only the ten trees, a few tiny roots at their bases. No ground patches, NO cast shadows detached from the objects, no backgrounds, no people, no buildings, no pools, no text, no labels, no grid lines, no watermarks.
Background must be true transparent RGBA. Leave generous completely transparent margins on every side of every tree. Preserve rich opaque colors on the tree silhouettes.
